# Hydration Guide for Footballers

## Introduction

Football is often played in heat and humidity with high sweat losses. Even mild dehydration (as little as 2% of body weight lost as sweat) can reduce sprint speed, endurance, and concentration. This guide summarizes practical hydration targets before, during, and after activity.

## Hydration Before Training or a Match

- Begin sessions well-hydrated rather than trying to "catch up" during play.
- **2-3 hours before:** drink roughly 5-10 ml of fluid per kg body weight (e.g., ~400-700 ml for a 70 kg player).
- **10-20 minutes before:** a small additional 200-300 ml if urine is still dark or infrequent.
- Water is sufficient for this stage unless the athlete is entering a long or hot session.

## Hydration During Training or a Match

- Aim to replace a large portion of ongoing sweat losses, generally **300-800 ml per hour**, adjusted for heat, humidity, and individual sweat rate.
- Use natural breaks (injuries, half-time, drinks breaks) to sip fluids rather than large single gulps.
- For sessions longer than ~60 minutes or in hot conditions, a drink containing carbohydrate (~4-8%) and electrolytes helps sustain energy and fluid balance.

| Session duration | Recommended fluid strategy |
|---|---|
| Under 60 minutes | Water is usually adequate |
| 60-90 minutes, or hot/humid conditions | Water + electrolytes; consider added carbohydrate |
| Over 90 minutes / tournament conditions | Carbohydrate-electrolyte drink recommended |

## Hydration After Training or a Match

- Rehydration should replace both sweat losses and continued urine losses.
- A practical target is drinking **~1.2-1.5 litres for every 1 kg of body weight lost** during the session.
- Weighing before and after training (when possible) helps estimate individual sweat loss.
- Include some sodium (electrolytes, or salted snacks) with post-session fluids to improve retention, especially after heavy-sweating sessions.

## Electrolyte Balance

- Sweat contains sodium, potassium, and smaller amounts of magnesium and calcium; heavy sweating without replacement can contribute to cramping and fatigue.
- Simple electrolyte sources: coconut water, buttermilk (chaas) with a pinch of salt, oral rehydration solution (ORS), or a banana plus salted snacks.
- Players who are "salty sweaters" (visible salt residue on skin/clothing after training) may need higher sodium intake around sessions.

## Dehydration Symptoms

Recognizing early signs allows correction before performance is affected:

- Thirst, dry mouth
- Dark yellow, strong-smelling urine and reduced urine frequency
- Fatigue, headache, or dizziness
- Reduced sprint speed and difficulty concentrating
- In more severe cases: cramping, nausea, or overheating
