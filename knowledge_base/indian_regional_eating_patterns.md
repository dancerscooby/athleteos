# Indian Regional Eating Patterns

## Introduction

India does not have one cuisine. What is ordinary, affordable and available differs sharply between regions, and a meal plan that ignores this produces food an athlete will not actually cook or eat. This document describes how people commonly eat across six broad zones, so an athlete's meals can be built from foods that are normal in their own region. See the section for the relevant zone below.

**Scope — cultural and food-availability information only.** Nothing here is a nutrition or health claim, and nothing here describes the people of a region. Location affects which foods are available, affordable and familiar. It says nothing about anyone's metabolism, body composition, genetics or nutritional requirements. Two athletes with the same body stats and training load have the same nutritional needs wherever they live; only the foods used to meet them differ. For targets see `football_nutrition.md`; for vegetarian micronutrient issues see `indian_vegetarian_nutrition_gaps.md`.

## North India
*Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand*

- **Staples:** whole-wheat roti at main meals, with rice alongside rather than replacing it. Missi and bajra roti in winter and in Rajasthan.
- **Typical day:** cooked breakfast (paratha, poha, besan chilla, daliya); substantial lunch of roti with dal or a legume curry plus vegetable sabzi; evening snack with tea; dinner similar to lunch.
- **Protein:** dal, rajma, chole, paneer; eggs and chicken for non-vegetarians. Dairy is prominent — curd, milk, lassi, ghee are everyday items.
- **Fats:** mustard oil, ghee, refined vegetable oils.
- Vegetarianism is widespread, especially in Rajasthan, Haryana and parts of UP.

## South India
*Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana*

- **Staples:** rice dominates. Fermented rice-and-lentil items (idli, dosa, uttapam) are everyday breakfasts, not special occasions. Ragi is common in Karnataka.
- **Typical day:** early cooked breakfast; rice lunch with sambar or rasam, a vegetable poriyal/thoran and curd; tea-time snack; lighter rice or dosa dinner.
- **Protein:** toor and urad dal (via sambar, rasam, idli/dosa batter), chana; fish on the coast, chicken and eggs inland.
- **Fats:** coconut oil (especially Kerala), groundnut and sesame oil.
- The fermented-batter tradition is practically relevant — see `indian_vegetarian_nutrition_gaps.md` for why fermentation matters.

## East India
*West Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Jharkhand*

- **Staples:** rice primarily; roti common in Bihar and Jharkhand. Litti is characteristic of Bihar.
- **Typical day:** light breakfast (chire, muri, sattu paratha, luchi); rice-and-dal lunch with vegetables and often fish; a snack such as jhalmuri or ghugni; rice-based dinner.
- **Protein:** cholar and moong dal, ghugni (white peas), sattu (roasted gram flour, used as food and as a cold drink); freshwater fish is a mainstay in Bengal and Odisha.
- **Fats:** mustard oil predominates.
- Fish is routine rather than occasional for many households, which changes what "affordable protein" means versus inland regions.

## West India
*Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa, coastal Konkan*

- **Staples:** jowar and bajra bhakri alongside wheat roti; rice common in coastal and Konkan areas.
- **Typical day:** poha, thepla, upma, dhokla or misal pav; lunch of bhakri/roti with dal, sprouted-bean usal or vegetable shaak; snack; similar dinner.
- **Protein:** dal, chana, sprouted legumes (matki, moong, val — sprouting is routine household practice), paneer; fish on the Konkan and Goan coast.
- **Fats:** groundnut oil, coconut oil on the coast.
- Gujarat has a strong vegetarian tradition; coastal Maharashtra and Goa are strongly fish-oriented.

## Central India
*Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh*

- **Staples:** wheat, jowar and maize rotis; rice more prominent in Chhattisgarh than in most of MP.
- **Typical day:** poha (Indore-style with sev is a regional signature), bhutte ka kees in maize season, or muthia in Chhattisgarh; roti-and-dal lunch, typically arhar dal; simple vegetable sabzis.
- **Protein:** arhar, chana and urad dal; soya is genuinely common — Madhya Pradesh is a major soybean-producing state, so soy chunks are inexpensive and widely available.
- **Fats:** groundnut oil, mustard oil, ghee.
- Often incorrectly treated as an extension of north India; its grain mix (more jowar and maize) and soy availability differ meaningfully.

## Northeast India
*Assam, Manipur, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim*

- **Staples:** rice, including sticky and black rice. Rice flakes are common at breakfast.
- **Typical day:** rice with boiled or lightly stir-fried vegetables and a small portion of fish or meat; preparations are typically boiled, steamed or lightly fermented rather than heavily spiced or deep-fried.
- **Protein:** freshwater and dried fish, pork (Nagaland, Mizoram), chicken, black gram and dals. Fermented ingredients are central: axone/akhuni (fermented soybean), bamboo shoot, fermented fish (ngari, berma).
- **Fats:** mustard oil; less oil overall than north Indian cooking.
- **Dairy is much less central here than in north or west India.** Curd, paneer and milk-based sides should not be assumed as defaults the way they reasonably can be in Punjab or Gujarat.

## Practical Implications

- Build plans from the region's own staple grain rather than defaulting to roti everywhere.
- Do not assume dairy is available or customary in every region.
- Coastal, riverine and northeastern athletes often have cheaper access to fish than inland athletes have to chicken or paneer.
- Fermentation, sprouting and soaking are already routine in several regions — build on what a household already does.
- Regional labels are broad approximations. Individual household practice and the athlete's stated preferences always override any regional generalisation.
