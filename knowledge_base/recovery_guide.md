# Post-Training and Post-Match Recovery Guide

## Introduction

Recovery determines how well a footballer adapts to training and how ready they are for the next session or match. Nutrition, sleep, and light physical practices (stretching, mobility work) all contribute. This guide summarizes the key recovery levers in the hours and days after exertion.

## Post-Match Recovery Overview

Recovery has multiple simultaneous goals after a hard session or match:

- Replenishing depleted muscle and liver glycogen (carbohydrate stores)
- Repairing muscle micro-damage (protein)
- Rehydrating and restoring electrolyte balance
- Reducing inflammation and muscle soreness
- Restoring mental freshness through sleep and downtime

## Protein Intake for Recovery

- Consume **20-30 g of protein** within about 30-60 minutes after finishing, when practical (this window is helpful but not rigidly strict).
- Good post-session options: curd, paneer, eggs, milk, soy chunks, or a mixed dal-rice meal.
- Continue spreading protein across meals for the rest of the day and the following day, since muscle repair continues for 24-48 hours.

## Glycogen Replenishment

- Glycogen stores can take **24-48 hours** to fully restore after an intense match, depending on carbohydrate intake.
- A carbohydrate-rich meal or snack in the first 1-2 hours post-match speeds this process; combining carbohydrate with protein (e.g., poha with peanuts and curd) works well.
- On the day after a match, maintain higher carbohydrate intake (see `football_nutrition.md` targets) rather than eating a "light" recovery-only diet.

## Sleep

- 7-9 hours per night supports hormonal recovery (growth hormone release is highest during deep sleep) and reduces injury risk.
- Consistent sleep and wake times help regulate recovery better than occasional long sleep-ins.
- Avoid heavy meals, caffeine, and screens close to bedtime after evening matches, as post-match adrenaline can already delay sleep onset.

## Stretching and Mobility

- Light stretching or mobility work after a session can help maintain range of motion and reduce stiffness, though its effect on next-day soreness is modest.
- Active recovery (an easy walk, light cycling, or swimming) the day after a match can support blood flow and feel better than complete rest for some players.
- Foam rolling or massage, where available, are commonly used to reduce perceived muscle tightness.

## Practical Recovery Checklist

| Time after match | Priority action |
|---|---|
| 0-30 min | Rehydrate; begin light carbohydrate + protein snack if tolerated |
| 30-60 min | Full recovery meal (carbohydrate + protein) |
| 2-4 hours | Balanced meal; continue fluids |
| Evening | Wind down, avoid heavy late meals, prioritize sleep |
| Next day | Light activity or rest, continue higher carbohydrate intake, monitor soreness/fatigue |
