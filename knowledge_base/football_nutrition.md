# Football Nutrition Fundamentals

## Introduction

Football (soccer) is an intermittent high-intensity sport combining aerobic endurance with repeated sprints, jumps, and direction changes over 90+ minutes. Nutrition needs are shaped by this mixed demand: sustained carbohydrate availability, adequate protein for muscle repair, and consistent hydration. This guide summarizes core principles for amateur and semi-professional footballers.

## Carbohydrates for Performance

Carbohydrate is the primary fuel for high-intensity actions (sprinting, jumping) and is stored in muscles and the liver as glycogen. Depleted glycogen is strongly linked to fatigue in the final third of a match.

- **Daily training days:** ~5-7 g carbohydrate per kg body weight.
- **Heavy training / match days:** ~7-10 g/kg.
- **Light training or rest days:** ~3-5 g/kg is usually sufficient.
- Prefer whole-food sources: rice, roti, oats, potatoes, fruit, and legumes over refined sugar.

| Training load | Carbohydrate target (g/kg body weight/day) |
|---|---|
| Light / recovery day | 3-5 |
| Moderate training | 5-7 |
| High-intensity / match day | 7-10 |

## Protein for Recovery

Protein supplies amino acids for repairing muscle damaged by training and matches, and supports adaptation between sessions.

- **General target:** ~1.4-1.8 g protein per kg body weight per day for athletes in regular training.
- Spread protein across meals (e.g., 20-30 g per meal) rather than concentrating it in one sitting.
- Combine plant sources (dal, soy, legumes) to cover the full amino acid profile if eating vegetarian.

## Healthy Fats

Fats are a secondary but essential fuel source and support hormone production and joint health.

- Aim for roughly 20-30% of total daily calories from fat.
- Favor unsaturated sources: nuts, seeds, peanut/mustard oil, ghee in moderation, and fatty fish (for non-vegetarians).
- Avoid heavy, fried, or very high-fat meals close to training or matches, as they slow digestion.

## Meal Timing

- **2-4 hours before training/match:** a balanced meal with carbohydrate, moderate protein, and low fat/fibre to ease digestion.
- **Within 30-60 minutes after training/match:** a carbohydrate + protein snack or meal to start glycogen refill and muscle repair (the "recovery window").
- Regular meal spacing (every 3-4 hours) helps maintain energy for players training multiple times per day.

## Hydration

- Fluid losses of even 2% of body weight can measurably impair performance and decision-making.
- Drink consistently through the day, not just around training.
- See `hydration_guide.md` for detailed before/during/after guidance.

## Sleep and Recovery

- 7-9 hours of sleep supports muscle repair, hormonal balance, and reaction time.
- Poor sleep is associated with higher injury risk and slower recovery between sessions.
- Short daytime naps (20-30 minutes) can supplement recovery during heavy training blocks.
