# Indian Protein Sources for Athletes

## Introduction

Protein is essential for muscle repair and adaptation, but imported supplements are often expensive or hard to access for many Indian footballers. This guide summarizes commonly available Indian foods that provide meaningful protein, along with their approximate cost, to support realistic, budget-aware meal planning.

Values below are **approximate** per 100 g of the food as commonly prepared/consumed, intended for educational estimation rather than precise lab-grade figures. Costs are rough retail approximations in India and vary by region, season, and brand.

## Vegetarian Protein Sources

| Food | Approx. protein (per 100 g) | Classification | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paneer | ~18 g | Vegetarian | Moderate (~Rs 350-450/kg) |
| Milk (full-fat) | ~3.5 g | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 55-65/litre) |
| Curd / yogurt | ~3.5-4 g | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 60-90/kg) |
| Soy chunks (dry) | ~50 g (dry weight) | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 150-200/kg) |
| Lentils / dal (cooked) | ~9 g | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 120-180/kg dry) |
| Chickpeas / chana (cooked) | ~19 g (dry weight basis) | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 90-130/kg dry) |
| Rajma / kidney beans (cooked) | ~24 g (dry weight basis) | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 130-180/kg dry) |
| Peanuts (roasted) | ~25 g | Vegetarian | Low (~Rs 130-180/kg) |

## Non-Vegetarian Protein Sources

| Food | Approx. protein (per 100 g) | Classification | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eggs | ~13 g (whole egg) | Non-vegetarian | Low (~Rs 6-8 per egg) |
| Chicken (breast/curry cut) | ~27 g | Non-vegetarian | Moderate (~Rs 200-280/kg) |
| Fish (e.g., rohu, pomfret varies) | ~18-22 g | Non-vegetarian | Moderate-High (~Rs 200-500/kg, species-dependent) |

## Notes on Practical Use

- **Dry-weight vs. cooked-weight:** legumes are commonly listed by dry weight since cooking increases water content and reduces protein density per 100 g of the cooked dish; always sanity-check portion sizes when planning meals.
- **Combining sources:** pairing grains (rice, roti) with legumes (dal, rajma, chana) provides a more complete amino acid profile for vegetarian athletes.
- **Budget stacking:** eggs, dal, curd, and peanuts are generally the most cost-effective protein sources per gram of protein in most Indian markets, useful for tight-budget meal plans.
- **Regional availability:** fish and specific legume varieties vary significantly by region; the table above is a general national reference, not exhaustive. See the regional breakdown below, and `indian_regional_eating_patterns.md` for fuller context.

## Regional Protein Availability

Which of the proteins above are cheap and easy to get depends heavily on where the athlete lives. The nutritional content of a food does not change by region — its **price, availability and cultural familiarity** do.

| Region | Everyday, low-cost protein | Commonly available | Less usual / costlier |
|---|---|---|---|
| North | Dal, rajma, chole/chana, curd, milk, paneer | Eggs, chicken, soy chunks, peanuts | Fish (inland areas) |
| South | Toor and urad dal (sambar, rasam, idli/dosa batter), curd, chana | Eggs, chicken, fish (coastal), peanuts, soy chunks | Paneer (less traditional, but available) |
| East | Cholar/moong dal, ghugni (white peas), sattu, freshwater fish | Eggs, chicken, curd, soy chunks | Paneer, mutton |
| West | Dal, chana, sprouted legumes (matki, moong, val), curd, paneer | Eggs, chicken, fish (Konkan/Goa coast), peanuts | — |
| Central | Arhar/chana/urad dal, **soy chunks** (Madhya Pradesh is a major soybean-producing state, so soy is notably cheap here), curd | Eggs, chicken, peanuts, milk | Fish |
| Northeast | Freshwater and dried fish, black gram, dal, fermented soybean (axone/akhuni) | Pork (Nagaland, Mizoram), chicken, eggs | **Dairy and paneer — much less central to everyday cooking here than in north or west India** |

Practical points:

- **Fish is often the cheapest quality protein** for athletes in Bengal, Odisha, coastal Maharashtra, Goa, Kerala, coastal Tamil Nadu and the northeast — where in inland regions the equivalent budget choice is usually dal plus eggs.
- **Do not assume dairy everywhere.** Curd and paneer are reasonable defaults in the north and west; in the northeast they are not, and a plan built around them may be neither familiar nor easily available.
- **Soy chunks are the cheapest concentrated protein in most of the country** and are especially inexpensive in central India.
- **Sattu** (roasted gram flour) is a distinctive, very cheap protein source in Bihar and eastern UP, usable as a drink or in parathas.
- **Sprouting is already routine** in western India, which makes sprouted-legume suggestions realistic rather than novel there.
- Regional patterns are broad generalisations. An individual athlete's stated preferences, budget, allergies and household practice always take precedence.
